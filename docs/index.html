Frequency-Guided Spatial-Frequency Collaborative Network for Unregistered Aerial Visible-to-Infrared Image Translation

Abstract

Thermal infrared imagery enables day-and-night perception from unmanned aerial vehicles, yet large infrared datasets are costly to collect. Visible-to-infrared translation offers a low-cost alternative, but it must be learned from unregistered data because airborne visible and infrared sensors are rarely aligned. The gap between the two modalities varies with frequency and with image region. Low-frequency intensities must be re-mapped from reflectance to thermal radiance, boundaries caused by material or temperature changes persist in both modalities, and many reflection textures have no thermal counterpart. Spatial-domain translators blur thermal boundaries, while uniform frequency guidance transfers visible textures into homogeneous regions.

As the contribution to artificial intelligence, we propose a frequency-guided spatial-frequency collaborative network (FSFNet) for unpaired translation. A Haar wavelet encoder separates low- and high-frequency representations, which act as queries in a frequency-guided cross-attention module to retrieve spatial context. A granularity-aware gating mechanism measures local structural complexity with parameter-free variance statistics and balances local smoothing against cross-window detail at each position. An auxiliary wavelet decoder used only in training anchors the shared bottleneck to infrared spectral structure without extra inference cost.

As the engineering application, FSFNet generates aerial infrared imagery and, on three public daytime and nighttime drone benchmarks, achieves the best learned perceptual image patch similarity and structural similarity on all three. On the Aerial Visible-to-Infrared Image Dataset (AVIID-3), it reduces the Fréchet inception distance by 11.5% and improves the peak signal-to-noise ratio by 0.65 dB over a spatial-only baseline.

Highlights
  • The visible-infrared gap in aerial scenes depends on frequency band and image region.
  • Wavelet frequency features act as queries that guide spatial translation.
  • Local structural statistics balance local and cross-window attention by region.
  • A training-only wavelet decoder keeps infrared spectral structure at no test cost.
  • Best perceptual and structural similarity on three public aerial benchmarks.

Motivation and Contributions

Unpaired aerial visible-to-infrared translation and the two failure types that motivate this work. Visible and infrared images are formed by reflected light and emitted radiance, and no pixel-level registration is available during training. Spatial-only translators blur thermal boundaries and lose high-frequency detail. Frequency guidance without regional regulation introduces spurious textures in smooth regions and leaves complex regions short of detail.

A visible camera records reflected sunlight, whereas an infrared camera records radiance emitted according to surface temperature and emissivity. The modality gap is therefore not uniform across frequency bands or image regions: the low-frequency layout must be re-mapped, structural boundaries should be preserved, and reflection-only textures should be suppressed. These observations define four requirements for unpaired translation: (i) separating the components that need different treatment, (ii) letting them guide translation rather than serve as side information, (iii) determining where high-frequency detail should be preserved, and (iv) retaining infrared spectral structure without paired targets.

The main contributions are:

  1. We formulate the visible-infrared gap in aerial scenes as frequency- and region-dependent and design a dual-path generator around frequency-guided cross-attention (FGCA), in which low- and high-frequency wavelet representations act as attention queries and swap between regular-window and shifted-window streams across layers. The frequency representations are also re-injected at every decoding scale.
  2. We propose the granularity-aware adaptive gating mechanism (GAGM), which adapts spatial-frequency fusion to regional complexity through parameter-free structural statistics and a progressive schedule. It reverses the degradation caused by unregulated frequency guidance in texture-rich daytime scenes and suppresses texture artifacts on homogeneous surfaces.
  3. We introduce a training-only auxiliary wavelet decoder that anchors the shared bottleneck to infrared spectral structure under unpaired supervision at no inference cost.
  4. Experiments on three public aerial benchmarks show that FSFNet ranks first in 11 of the 18 metric evaluations and achieves the best LPIPS and SSIM on all datasets. Ablation studies show that the three components are complementary: the auxiliary decoder lowers the nighttime FID of the dual-path model from 58.47 to 19.23, and GAGM further improves all six metrics on all three datasets.

Method

Overall architecture of FSFNet. The spatial encoder Es and the Haar wavelet frequency encoder Ef extract complementary representations in parallel. FGCA uses the low- and high-frequency representations as queries to retrieve spatial context, and GAGM regulates the two attention streams by local structural complexity. The decoder Ds re-injects the frequency representations at every scale, and the auxiliary wavelet decoder Df is used only during training.

RequirementComponentRole
(i) Separate the componentsHaar wavelet frequency encoder EfTwo-level Haar DWT with an independent branch per subband; LL features form Flow, and LH/HL/HH features form Fhigh
(ii) Let them guide translationFGCAFlow and Fhigh serve as queries over spatial keys and values in regular- and shifted-window streams, and swap streams across layers
(iii) Decide where detail is preservedGAGMA parameter-free variance-based granularity map weights the two streams at each position, phased in by a progressive schedule
(iv) Retain infrared spectral structureAuxiliary wavelet decoder DfReconstructs real infrared images from predicted Haar subbands in the identity pass; removed at inference

Structure of FGCA and GAGM. The low- and high-frequency representations serve as queries for cross-attention on regular (W-MCA) and shifted (SW-MCA) windows, and their window assignment alternates across layers. GAGM weights the two attention streams with a channel gate and a granularity-guided position gate.

Two-stage training strategy of FSFNet. Stage 1 pretrains the generator by masked image modeling with pixel and wavelet losses. Stage 2 learns the unpaired translation with adversarial, geometric-consistency, identity, and auxiliary losses. The auxiliary wavelet decoder is removed at inference.

Results

Over the 18 metric evaluations on the three benchmarks, FSFNet ranks first in 11 and within the top two in 16, and it achieves the best LPIPS and SSIM on all three datasets. All methods are trained from scratch with their public code under the same data splits, input resolution, and evaluation protocol. Best results are in bold and second-best are underlined; ↓ (↑) indicates that lower (higher) is better.

FSFNet translates a 256 × 256 image in 36.14 ms (about 27.7 images per second) with a single forward pass, and the auxiliary decoder adds no inference cost because it is removed after training. This throughput supports efficient large-scale generation of infrared training data for aerial perception tasks.
